Skeletal and muscular systems

The skeleton

Four jobs

  • Support: holds the body up and gives it shape.
  • Protection: the cranium (part of the skull) protects the brain. The ribs protect the heart and lungs.
  • Movement: bones are pulled by muscles at joints.
  • Making blood cells: red blood cells are made in the bone marrow.

Inside a joint

Ligaments, tendons and cartilage

  • Ligament: joins bone to bone. Keeps the joint together.
  • Tendon: joins muscle to bone. Passes the pull of the muscle to the bone, so the bone moves.
  • Cartilage: smooth, rubbery covering on the ends of bones. Stops them rubbing and cushions the joint.

If a tendon is injured, the muscle cannot pull on the bone properly. That makes the body part hard to move, for example an athlete trying to run.

Muscles

Muscles only pull

A muscle can contract (get shorter and pull) and relax (go back). It cannot push. So muscles work in pairs called antagonistic pairs. As one contracts, the other relaxes.

Data skills

Reading results

  • Find the highest or lowest value first. Say which week or which row it is.
  • Describe the trend: does it go up, go down, or stay the same? Say when it changes.
  • Use numbers from the table as evidence, and include the units (N for newtons).
  • A sudden big change tells you something happened at that point.

A cell is a tiny living unit. It is the building block of all living things.

Levels of organisation

Cells make up systems

cell→tissue→organ→organ system→organism
  • A tissue is a group of similar cells doing the same job, for example muscle tissue.
  • An organ is made of different tissues working together, for example the stomach.
  • An organ system is a group of organs working together, for example the digestive system.
Microscope

Using a light microscope

  • Eyepiece lens: the part you look through.
  • Objective lenses: magnify the specimen. Different strengths.
  • Stage: flat surface where you put the slide.
  • Focusing wheel: turn it to get a clear image.
  • Light source: shines light through the slide.
  • A stain (for example methylene blue) makes parts of cells easier to see.

Spot a plant cell

Look for a cell wall, a large vacuole, chloroplasts, and a regular shape.

Unicellular organism

Euglena

  • Chloroplasts let it make food by photosynthesis.
  • The flagellum is a whip-like tail. It moves the cell through water so it can reach light and food.
  • The eye spot detects light.
Unicellular organism

Amoeba and diffusion

Diffusion is the movement of particles from where there are lots of them (high concentration) to where there are fewer (low concentration).

An amoeba is one cell, so everything it needs must pass through its membrane. Oxygen and nutrients diffuse in. Waste such as carbon dioxide diffuses out. It needs oxygen and nutrients for respiration, to release energy.
